Study in Japan from Bangladesh: MEXT and the Route Most Students Miss

Japan is the destination our students rule out for the wrong reason. They assume it means learning Japanese to fluency first, and that the money is out of reach. Neither is quite true, and Japan quietly funds more Bangladeshi students than most people here realise.

MEXT, and the two ways in

MEXT is the Japanese government's scholarship for international students, run by the Ministry of Education. It is generous by the standards of anything else on the table: tuition covered, a monthly allowance, and airfare.

There are two routes to it and students constantly conflate them.

The embassy recommendation route runs through the Embassy of Japan in Bangladesh. They publish scholarship notices on their own site with the year's categories, eligibility and deadlines. You apply through the embassy, sit written tests and an interview in Dhaka, and if you are selected you are placed at a Japanese university.

The university recommendation route goes through a Japanese university that has already accepted you or is willing to nominate you. Here you are dealing with the institution and often with a specific professor, particularly for research and master's places.

If you are finishing HSC or a bachelor's and have strong results, the embassy route is usually where you start. If you are targeting a research degree and can find a supervisor whose work matches yours, the university route is often the stronger play.

Watch the embassy's notices page rather than relying on an agency to tell you when applications open. The window is fixed and it does not move for anybody.

The language question

For undergraduate MEXT, expect Japanese to be part of the deal, usually with a preparatory year of intensive language study before your degree begins. You are not expected to arrive fluent, you are expected to learn.

At postgraduate level, a large number of programmes are taught in English, especially in engineering, science and some business fields. You can complete a master's or PhD in English.

Daily life is a different matter. Japan functions in Japanese, and the students who settle well are the ones who treat language study as part of the plan rather than a hurdle to clear once.

Beyond MEXT

Not getting MEXT does not close the door. Many Japanese universities have their own tuition reductions and scholarships for international students, and several national universities charge fees that compare well with the UK or Australia once you account for the exchange rate.

There are also JASSO-administered supports and regional scholarships. The catch is that these are typically decided after admission, so you need to be able to show funds for your visa regardless.

What Japan asks of you

Precision. Japanese admissions and visa processes reward applications that are complete, accurate and submitted on time, and they are unforgiving about missing pieces. If your instinct is to send an incomplete file now and follow up later, Japan will punish that.

Your documents also need to line up exactly. Names, dates, certificate numbers. The same discipline you need for any visa file, with less tolerance for slippage.

Who it suits

Students with genuinely strong academic records who are willing to learn a language and who want a degree that carries weight across Asia. Also students in engineering and the sciences, where Japan's research funding is real and access to it is a serious reason to go.

It suits students looking for a quick, low-effort route abroad very badly.

Before you start

Find the current scholarship notice on the Embassy of Japan in Bangladesh's own website, not a summary somebody posted in a Facebook group. Check the category, the eligibility and the deadline, then work backwards.
