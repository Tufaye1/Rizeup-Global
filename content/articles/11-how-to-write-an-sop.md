The Statement of Purpose (SOP) is the one part of your application that's entirely in your control — and it can tip a borderline decision in your favour, for both admissions and the visa. Here's how to write one that works.

## What an SOP actually is

An SOP is a short essay explaining **who you are, why this course, why this university, and where you're heading**. Admissions officers read hundreds — a good SOP is specific, honest and focused, not a list of adjectives about yourself.

## A structure that works

1. **Hook + goal** — open with a genuine reason you're drawn to the field, and your goal.
2. **Academic background** — what you've studied and achieved that's relevant.
3. **Why this course** — what you want to learn and why it fits your goal.
4. **Why this university** — specific programs, professors, or strengths (this shows real research).
5. **Career plan** — what you'll do after, ideally tied back to home.
6. **Close** — a confident, concise wrap-up.

## What makes an SOP stand out

- **Specifics over clichés** — "I led a 4-person robotics project" beats "I am passionate and hardworking."
- **A clear thread** — every paragraph should support your goal.
- **Genuine university research** — name real programs or faculty, not generic praise.
- **Your own voice** — authentic and readable, not stuffed with big words.

## The SOP for a student visa

A **visa SOP** is related but different: it reassures the officer that you're a genuine student with clear intent and the funds to study, and that you plan to return. Keep it factual, consistent with your application, and specific about your course and finances.

## Common SOP mistakes

- Being **generic** — an SOP that could be copy-pasted to any university.
- **Repeating your CV** instead of telling a story.
- **Exaggerating** or including anything you can't back up.
- **Ignoring word limits** and structure.
- Writing the visa SOP and university SOP as if they're identical.
