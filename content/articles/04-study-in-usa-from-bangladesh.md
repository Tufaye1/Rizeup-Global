The USA has the widest range of universities in the world — from the Ivy League to affordable state universities that are generous with scholarships. That range is exactly why it rewards good planning. Here is how Bangladeshi students can approach it.

## Why the USA?

American universities offer flexibility (you can explore subjects before specialising), world-class research, and some of the largest scholarship budgets anywhere. There is a genuine option for almost every profile and budget if you look beyond the famous names.

## Choosing from the universities in the USA

Because there are thousands of universities, the goal is to build a smart shortlist rather than chase a single list. Balance three tiers: ambitious "reach" schools, solid "match" schools that fit your profile, and affordable "safety" options. Many strong public universities — such as large state universities known for value and scholarships — are excellent choices for international students.

## Scholarships to study in the USA

This is where the USA shines for well-prepared applicants. Many universities offer merit scholarships to international students that can cut tuition substantially, and some are effectively need-aware. Applying early, with a strong SOP and recommendation letters, is the difference between a full-price offer and a heavily funded one.

## Cost of studying in the USA

Costs vary enormously. Tuition at many public universities **starts around $15,000/yr**, while private and top-ranked schools cost far more before aid. Always look at the *net* cost after scholarships — a "cheaper" university with no aid can end up costing more than a pricier one that funds you well.

## Do you need IELTS? MOI-accepted options

Most US universities require IELTS or TOEFL, but a growing number are **MOI-accepted** — they'll consider an English-medium-of-instruction letter in place of a test. If you do need a test, we'll tell you the exact score your target universities want.

## The application process

1. Build a balanced shortlist (reach / match / safety).
2. Prepare transcripts, English scores, SOP, and recommendation letters.
3. Submit applications — watch for those with **no application fee**.
4. Compare offers by net cost, accept, and apply for the F-1 student visa with proper interview prep.

Plan for roughly **6–9 months** including the visa, and start early.
