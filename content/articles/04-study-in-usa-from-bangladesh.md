No country gives you more choice than the US, and that's the whole point. Thousands of universities, from the Ivy League to state schools that hand out real money. That range rewards planning and punishes guesswork. Here's how to approach it.

## Why the US

You can try subjects before you commit, the research money is serious, and scholarship budgets are the biggest you'll find anywhere if you look past the famous names. There's a fit for almost any profile. You just have to build toward it.

## Picking from thousands

Nobody applies to "the best US university." You build a smart list in three tiers: a couple of reaches, a few matches that suit your profile, and safe options you're confident of. The quiet winners are the big state universities, well regarded and often generous with aid.

## Scholarships to study in the USA

This is where a prepared applicant cleans up. Many universities cut tuition hard for strong international students, and some weigh your finances too. Apply early, with a real SOP and solid recommendation letters, and a full-price offer can turn into a funded one. Late and generic gets you neither.

## What it costs

The range is wild. Public universities often start near $15,000 a year, while private and top-ranked ones cost far more before aid. Always read the net, not the sticker. A "cheap" university that gives you nothing can end up dearer than a pricey one that funds you well.

## Do you need IELTS?

Most US universities want IELTS or TOEFL, but a growing number are MOI-friendly and will take an English-medium letter instead. If you do need a test, we'll tell you the exact score your targets ask for so you're not shooting in the dark.

## Applying

1. Build the reach, match and safety list.
2. Prep transcripts, English score, SOP and references.
3. Apply, and hunt down the ones with no application fee.
4. Compare offers by net cost, accept, then prep hard for the F-1 visa interview.

Give it six to nine months with the visa, and start early.
