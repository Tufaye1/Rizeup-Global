"Can you really get me a scholarship?" is the question we hear most. The honest answer: no one can *promise* a scholarship — but funding is far more common than students think, and applying properly is what separates a full-price offer from a funded one. Here's how it works.

## Types of scholarships to study abroad

- **Merit scholarships** — for strong academic results (the most common). Tuition waivers of **40% or more** are realistic in Malaysia for good HSC grades.
- **Need-based aid** — some universities, especially in the USA, consider your financial situation.
- **Full scholarships** — cover most or all costs; highly competitive and deadline-driven.
- **Country and government scholarships** — some nations and universities run specific schemes for international or South Asian students.

## Can you get a free scholarship to study abroad?

Full-ride scholarships exist, but they are rare and extremely competitive. A more realistic path for most students is stacking a **strong merit scholarship** with an affordable country (like Malaysia or Germany) so your total cost drops dramatically — sometimes to less than studying at home.

## How to qualify

Scholarships reward preparation, not just grades:

- Solid **HSC / A-Level** results (and IELTS where required — a good band can unlock IELTS-linked awards).
- A compelling **Statement of Purpose** that shows direction, not just ambition.
- Clean, complete **documentation** submitted **before deadlines**.
- Applying to universities where your profile is competitive — fit matters.

## Where to look for scholarships

Start with the **universities themselves** — most international scholarships are offered directly by universities at the point of admission, not through separate portals. Beyond that, look at government schemes and reputable databases. Be cautious of anyone charging fees to "guarantee" a scholarship.

## How to apply for a scholarship abroad

1. Shortlist universities where you're a strong candidate.
2. Note each scholarship's criteria and deadline (many are automatic with admission; some need a separate form).
3. Build a strong application — SOP, transcripts, references, English score.
4. Apply early. Late applications miss the best-funded rounds.
