Studying abroad is one of the biggest decisions you will make — and for most Bangladeshi students, the hardest part is simply knowing where to start. This guide walks you through the whole journey, from choosing a country to landing on campus, in plain language.

## Why study abroad?

A degree from a recognised university abroad opens doors: better teaching, global exposure, post-study work rights in many countries, and a qualification employers respect worldwide. For many students it is also more affordable than people assume — some countries cost less per year than a private university at home once you factor in scholarships and part-time work.

## Which country should you choose?

There is no single "best" country — only the best fit for your grades, budget and goals. As a quick starting point:

- **Malaysia** — the most affordable and closest to home, English-taught, many programs accept MOI letters instead of IELTS.
- **Australia** — top-ranked universities and strong post-study work rights; plan a larger budget.
- **USA** — the widest range of universities and scholarships, from Ivy League to affordable state schools.
- **Germany** — very low or free public tuition; great for engineering and sciences.
- **UK** — fast one-year master's degrees and globally respected names.
- **Europe (Italy, France & more)** — low public tuition and growing English-taught options.

We cover each of these in its own detailed guide, linked at the end.

## What does it cost?

Budget for two things: **tuition** and **living costs**. As a rough guide, tuition starts around **$3,500/yr in Malaysia**, **$15,000/yr at many US public universities**, and **$22,000/yr in Australia**, while Germany's public universities can be close to free. Living costs vary just as much by city. Scholarships, MOI-friendly admissions and part-time work can bring the real number down a lot — a counsellor can map your actual total against your family budget.

## What do you need to apply?

Most universities look at your whole profile, not one number. Typically you will need:

- Your **HSC / A-Level results** (many Malaysian universities accept a 3.5+ GPA; Australia, the USA and Europe weigh your full profile).
- **English proof** — IELTS for Australia, the USA and most of Europe; for Malaysia, many universities accept MOI letters or their own placement test.
- A strong **Statement of Purpose (SOP)** and academic transcripts.
- **Financial proof** for the student visa stage.

Weak in one area? The right university choice can weigh the areas where you are strong.

## The step-by-step process

1. **Shortlist** universities that fit your grades, budget and course.
2. **Prepare documents** — transcripts, SOP, recommendation letters, English scores.
3. **Apply** and track each application to an offer.
4. **Accept an offer** and arrange finances.
5. **Apply for the student visa** with the right documents and interview prep.
6. **Pre-departure** — forex, SIM, accommodation, packing and your day-one plan.

Starting early is the single biggest advantage. If you finished HSC this year, begin now rather than after results season.

## Scholarships are more common than you think

You don't have to be top of your class to get help. Tuition waivers of 40% or more are common in Malaysia for strong HSC results, and many countries offer merit and need-based scholarships for international students. The key is matching your profile to scholarships you are realistically eligible for — and applying properly and on time.

## Do you need an agency or counsellor?

You can do it alone, but a good counsellor saves you from the expensive mistakes: wrong university choices, missed deadlines, weak SOPs and avoidable visa refusals. Look for someone who is transparent about fees, honest about your chances, and available at every step — not a call centre that hands you off.
