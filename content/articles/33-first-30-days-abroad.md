Everything up to boarding gets planned to death. The month after you land gets almost no thought, and it is the month that sets up everything else.

A student in one of the Bangladeshi forums put it better than we could: before you go, learn to cook and look after yourself. In Bangladesh you study hard because it is competitive. You do not necessarily learn to run a household while doing it. Abroad you have to do both at once, and the studying does not pause while you work it out.

## Week one, the boring admin that unlocks everything

There is a dependency chain here and it catches people out. You usually need an address to open a bank account. You need a bank account to get paid. You need a tax or insurance number before an employer will put you on the payroll. Each step takes days, and they have to happen in order.

So do them in your first week, not your third. Register with the university, get your student ID and proof of enrolment, get whatever national number your country issues, open the bank account, and get a local SIM.

The SIM first, actually. Everything else needs a local number for verification.

Students who drift through the first fortnight enjoying themselves are the ones still waiting on a bank account in week five, watching their taka savings drain at a terrible exchange rate.

## Money, and the mistake almost everyone makes

Your first month is the most expensive one you will have. Deposit, first rent, bedding, kitchen basics, a winter coat if you have landed somewhere cold, a travel pass. None of this is in the budget people build, because the budget was built from tuition plus monthly rent.

Carry more than you think for the first month. Two or three months of living costs accessible immediately is the safe version, and it is also the version that stops you taking a bad job out of panic.

Learn the supermarket. In most destinations, the difference between eating out and cooking is the difference between a viable budget and an impossible one. This is the single biggest controllable cost in your student life.

## Cooking is not optional

Take this seriously before you fly. Learn six meals you can make without thinking, including at least two that reheat well.

Rice, daal, a chicken curry you can batch, eggs, a pasta, something with vegetables. If you can cook those on autopilot, you will eat properly through exam weeks. If you cannot, you will live on takeaways, and it will cost you both money and health inside two months.

Bring your spices. This sounds trivial and every student who did not bring them regrets it in week three, when the nearest place selling them is an hour away and charging four times Dhaka prices.

## Homesickness arrives late

The first two weeks are usually fine. There is too much happening. It tends to hit somewhere around week four or five, when the novelty has gone and the routine has not formed yet, often right as the first assignment deadline lands.

Knowing that it is coming helps more than you would think. It is a normal phase, not a sign you made a mistake.

Two things that genuinely help. Build one routine that is yours in the new place, whether that is a gym, a prayer time, a weekly call home at a fixed hour. And make at least one friend who is not Bangladeshi. The Bangladeshi community abroad is a real support and you should use it, but if it becomes your entire social world, the country stays foreign for years.

## Academics work differently

Expect less spoon-feeding. Reading lists are yours to get through, attendance may be tracked but nobody will chase you, and deadlines are usually absolute in a way they often are not here.

Two habits worth forming immediately: go to office hours in the first month, even with nothing urgent to ask, because being a face rather than a name matters when you later need an extension or a reference. And find out how plagiarism is defined at your institution, properly. Referencing norms differ from what many students are used to here, and academic misconduct cases against international students are more common than anyone admits. Most of them are ignorance, not dishonesty. The penalty does not care which.

## A short first-week list

Local SIM. University registration and student ID. National insurance or tax number. Bank account. Register with a doctor if your country expects it. Transport card. Find the cheap supermarket and the nearest place selling spices. Work out your route to campus before your first class.

Then cook something.
