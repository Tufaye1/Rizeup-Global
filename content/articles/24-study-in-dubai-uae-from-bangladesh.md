Dubai rarely comes up when students list where they want to go, and then somebody mentions that you can take a UK degree there, three and a half hours from Dhaka, and the room gets interested.

The branch campus model is the whole story here, so let us be precise about what it is.

## What a branch campus actually is

Several Western universities run full campuses in the UAE, particularly in Dubai and Abu Dhabi. British, Australian and a few American institutions all have a presence.

At the better ones you study the same curriculum as the home campus and the degree you receive is issued by the home university. Not a franchise certificate, not a partner qualification. The same parchment as the students in Manchester or Wollongong.

That is the appeal in one line: a Western degree without a Western visa decision, at a lower total cost, close to home.

The caveat is that "same degree" is a claim you must verify per university and per programme, because it is not uniformly true. Check on the home university's own website, and check whether the UAE regulator has accredited the specific programme, not just the campus.

## Who this genuinely suits

Students who have been refused a UK or Australian visa and need a route that does not depend on winning that argument again.

Students whose families are in the Gulf already, which is a large number of Bangladeshi families.

Female students whose parents are uneasy about the West but comfortable with the UAE. We see this often and there is no point pretending otherwise.

Students who want to start now. Intakes are frequent and the process is usually faster than a Western visa cycle.

Students who intend to work in the Gulf afterwards, where a Dubai-based degree and local networks are worth more than they would be back home.

## What it does not give you

A migration pathway. The UAE is not a route to permanent residency in the way Canada or Australia can be. Student and work visas there are tied to sponsorship and they are renewals rather than a road to citizenship. If your real goal is to settle permanently in a Western country, going to Dubai first may be a detour rather than a step.

The cheapest option available. Dubai undercuts studying in the UK, but tuition is not low, and living costs in Dubai are meaningfully higher than in Malaysia. Do the total-cost arithmetic before assuming it is the budget choice.

The full campus experience of the home university. Branch campuses are smaller, with fewer programmes and a narrower range of societies and facilities. For some students that is fine. For others it matters more than they expected.

## The practical side

Your visa is typically sponsored by the university and tied to your enrolment, so your status depends on staying enrolled. Understand the attendance rules before you arrive.

Work rights are limited and more restricted than in Canada or Australia. Do not build a budget that assumes part-time earnings.

Check the accreditation of your specific programme with the UAE's Ministry of Education, and check with the home university that the degree is theirs.

## How to compare it properly

Put Dubai next to Malaysia rather than next to London, because that is the real comparison for most of our students. Malaysia is cheaper. Dubai is closer to the Gulf job market and more often gives you a Western-badged degree.

Then put it against the Western country you actually wanted, with honest numbers, including the risk and cost of a refusal.
