Every year around results time we have the same conversation. A student walks in, says "I want to go to Canada", and we ask two questions that change the whole plan. What did you actually get in HSC, and what can your family genuinely put behind this?

Nobody enjoys those questions. They save people a year.

## Start with the budget, not the brochure

Work out one number before anything else: what your family can commit for the first year, including tuition, living costs, the visa, the flight and a buffer. Not what they could raise in an emergency. What they can commit without selling something they need.

That number sorts destinations faster than any other factor.

The gap between the cheapest and the most expensive serious option is not small. Malaysia runs at a fraction of what Canada asks you to show before you even apply, and Canada wants CAN$23,448 a year in living costs on top of tuition. If your number is well below what a destination requires, that destination is not on your list this year. It might be in two years, after a diploma and some savings, and that is a real plan rather than a disappointment.

## Then your results

Be blunt with yourself here, because the officer reading your file will be.

Strong HSC results with good English open most doors, including scholarship routes in Korea, Japan and China that a lot of students never consider because they assume everything worth having is in the West.

Middling results narrow it. You are looking at good private universities in Malaysia, foundation or pathway programmes elsewhere, and destinations that weigh your overall profile rather than a single number.

Weak results do not end it, but they change the shape. A diploma or foundation year first, then a transfer or a bachelor's, is a slower road that actually arrives somewhere. Applying straight to a country that will refuse you is a fast road to nowhere.

## Then what you want afterwards

This is the question students skip and it matters more than the ranking of the university.

If you want to come home and work in Bangladesh, the brand of the degree and the cost matter most. Spending four times more for a marginally better-known name rarely pays back here.

If you want to settle abroad permanently, that points at Canada and Australia, where the student-to-residency path is a designed route rather than a hope. Understand that those are also the destinations with the highest financial bar and the most scrutiny.

If you want to work in the Gulf, a UAE degree and Gulf networks may beat a UK one.

If you want research, Japan, Korea, Germany and China are funded in ways that should be on your list.

## A rough way to sort yourself

Take your budget number and your results together.

Limited budget and decent results: look hard at fully funded scholarships in Korea, Japan and China, and at Malaysia as a base. This combination is the most underserved by agencies here, because commissions are smaller, which is exactly why students rarely hear about it.

Moderate budget and decent results: Malaysia, Europe with low public tuition, the UAE branch campuses.

Strong budget and strong results: the UK, Australia, Canada and the US are genuinely open, and then the question becomes what you want afterwards.

Strong budget and weak results: do not buy your way into a place you will struggle in. A foundation year or a diploma first protects the investment.

## The trap to avoid

Picking the country because a cousin went there, or because an agent gets the best commission from it. Both happen constantly. Your cousin had different grades, a different budget and a different plan.

The second trap is applying everywhere at once. Four half-built applications lose to one strong one, and refusals follow you.

## What to do this month

Write down your budget number. Get your HSC transcript and an honest read of your English level. Decide whether you want to return or stay. Those three things produce a shortlist of two or three countries, and then the work is choosing between them properly.
