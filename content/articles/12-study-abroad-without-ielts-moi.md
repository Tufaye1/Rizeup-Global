Not everyone needs IELTS to study abroad. Many universities accept a **Medium of Instruction (MOI)** letter instead — proof that your previous education was in English. Here's exactly how it works and where it applies.

## What is an MOI letter?

An MOI (Medium of Instruction) letter is an official document from your school or college stating that your studies were **taught and examined in English**. For students from English-medium backgrounds in Bangladesh, it can replace an English test at many universities.

## Which universities are MOI-accepted?

MOI acceptance is most common in:

- **Malaysia** — a large number of universities accept MOI letters, and many that don't offer their own English placement test.
- **The USA** — a growing number of universities are MOI-accepted or test-flexible for eligible applicants.
- Some universities in **other countries**, case by case.

Acceptance varies by university *and* by program, so the letter that works for one course may not work for another. Always confirm before you rely on it.

## When you still need IELTS

For **Australia, the UK and most of Europe**, IELTS (or an equivalent) is usually required for the university and often for the visa. Even where MOI is accepted for admission, a visa may still ask for a test in some countries. If you do need IELTS, aim for the exact band your target universities require — not a guess.

## MOI vs IELTS: which is better?

If you qualify for MOI and your target universities accept it, it **saves time and test fees**. But IELTS keeps *every* option open, including countries and universities that don't accept MOI, and it can unlock IELTS-linked scholarships. For many students, the smart move is to shortlist MOI-accepted universities first — and take IELTS only if your plan needs it.

## How to use MOI in your application

1. Confirm each target university accepts MOI **for your specific program**.
2. Request the MOI letter from your school/college in the correct format.
3. Keep IELTS as a backup if any shortlisted university requires it.
